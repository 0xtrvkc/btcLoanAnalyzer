BTC quantity vs. dollar value

Selling collateral always reduces the original pot when debt is positive. No finite price lets that pot alone keep its original BTC quantity. The recovery price above includes the BTC bought with your loan in the final wallet, but excludes it from the starting benchmark.

BTC sold = (principal + interest) ÷ target price
Final BTC = original BTC − BTC sold + loan-funded BTC
BTC recovery price = (principal + interest) ÷ loan-funded BTC

Net P/L = remaining BTC value + unused cash − original collateral value at borrowing. P/L % uses that original collateral value. Unused loan cash is counted once in net assets; it is neither spent on repayment nor converted into BTC.

Conditional on no earlier liquidation and a lender allowing collateral sale for repayment. Simple interest follows your APR and holding period. Fees, slippage and tax are excluded. Change Target BTC price to explore repayment without resizing the loan.

READ WITH RESULTS

Loan sizing: a new loan is sized from your reference price and initial LTV. This is a scenario planner; existing loan debt must not be inferred by changing today’s price.

Interest: simple APR × months ÷ 12, accrued into the horizon debt. Displayed “now” thresholds use principal only. No trading fees, liquidation fees, tax, slippage, or compounding are included.

Barrier probability: 2Φ[ln(liquidation price ÷ reference price) ÷ (σ√(90/365))], assuming constant volatility and zero log-price drift. It estimates touching a fixed principal-only barrier, and excludes jumps and interest-driven barrier changes. It is not a calibrated forecast.

Cycle score & sizing: the score uses heuristic weights, not backtested success probabilities. The retained half-Kelly illustration maps the score to an assumed win probability; it is not an optimal or recommended LTV.

Comparison assumptions

Consistent returns: loan return uses pledged collateral value. Futures portfolio return uses spot value plus posted margin. Futures-leg return uses only the futures P/L and its margin.

Liquidation: the simplified isolated-long threshold is entry × (1 − 1/leverage) ÷ (1 − maintenance margin). Funding deductions, exchange margin tiers, fees, and changing maintenance requirements can move it. Targets below a liquidation threshold are shown as unavailable, because the open-position comparison no longer applies.

Zero deployment: no futures position is opened. There is no futures funding, margin requirement, or futures liquidation price.

Scenario estimates, not execution prices. Model targets are uncertain; loan terms, fees, and liquidation paths can change the outcome. Ready
